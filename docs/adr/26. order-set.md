# 26. The complete order set

Status: Accepted

## Context

Until now an agent could place, list and cancel sandbox orders, read one quote at a time, and see positions and funds. It could not change a waiting order, check one order or read its fills, price a watchlist in one call, see the order book, place a multi-leg position as one set, ask what margin the broker would want, or get flat in a hurry. OpenAlgo offers all of these, and we follow its behaviour, re-derived and not copied (it is AGPL; we read it for behaviour only). Where its behaviour is a defect we say so and do something else.

## Decision

**Changing an order** (`modify_order`, `PATCH /orders/{id}`):
- Only a PENDING order, and only its quantity, limit price (LIMIT and SL) and trigger (SL and SL-M), as OpenAlgo allows. Type, side, instrument and product stay; changing them is a cancel and a new order.
- A change never fills. An order changed to a price the market has already crossed fills on the execution engine's next pass, at the latest price.
- The margin held follows the change: the old reserve is released and the new terms reserved in the same transaction. A change the funds don't cover is refused, and the order and funds stay as they were. OpenAlgo leaves the reserve at its first value; we don't copy that.
- A changed trigger disarms an SL order already armed.
- A change that grows the quantity goes through placing's intraday cutoff and capital cap. A price or trigger change, or a smaller quantity, adds no exposure and skips them.
- The engine fills or arms an order only if it is still what the engine matched. `SandboxPort.fill_pending` and `arm_pending` take the order as it was read; if a change landed in between, nothing happens and the next price decides.

**Reading orders**: one order's status (`GET /orders/{id}`) and today's trades (`GET /trades`, since 00:00 exchange time, newest first).

**Prices**:
- `get_quotes` (`POST /quotes`) takes up to 50 symbol and exchange pairs and makes one broker call. As in OpenAlgo, an instrument asked for twice is quoted once. Unknown or unquoted instruments are listed with the reason, and the call fails only when none is known.
- A quote carries best bid and ask with their quantities, the day's open, high and low, the previous close and volume. An absent price is None, never 0.
- `get_market_depth` (`GET /depth`) returns up to five levels a side, each with its order count. Empty levels are dropped.
- Depth totals are the broker's whole-book buy and sell quantities. OpenAlgo sums the top five levels, which can be a small fraction of the book; we don't copy that.
- Quotes and depth read the order book through one helper, so their best bid and ask always agree.

**Baskets** (`place_basket`, `POST /orders/basket`, up to 50):
- Every BUY is placed before any SELL, as OpenAlgo does, so a spread holds its hedge before it sells.
- Orders go through `place_order` one at a time. The set is not atomic: an order refused (unknown symbol, closed market, short of funds) is reported with its reason, and the rest still go.
- Results come back in placing order.

**Getting flat**:
- `close_position` places a MARKET order for exactly the quantity held. That quantity is read inside the same write transaction that records the fill, so an order filling meanwhile can't make the close go past flat. OpenAlgo reads the position first and then places an ordinary order; we don't copy that gap.
- Closing a position that isn't held is a 404.
- `close_all_positions` closes each position at the market, with prices from one quote call. One that can't close (exchange closed, no fresh price, expired contract) says why, and the rest still close.
- `cancel_all_orders` withdraws every PENDING order.
- As in OpenAlgo, none of these stops a strategy. A runner that later finds its leg flat records it as closed outside the strategy. A signal strategy may enter again on its next alert; the kill switch (ADR 12) is what stops that.

**Margin** (`get_margin`, `POST /margin`, up to 50 orders): the broker's own figure; nothing is placed.
- For Zerodha, one order is priced by `/margins/orders` and several by `/margins/basket?consider_positions=true`, as OpenAlgo does.
- The total is Kite's `final` figure, the one its own basket screen shows.
- The benefit is `initial` less `final`. Kite's `initial` is already partly offset, so placing the orders one by one would need more than total plus benefit.
- Every order must be known and well formed, or the call fails naming which one. OpenAlgo skips unknown symbols silently, which would price a hedged set without one of its legs.
- The sandbox still blocks margin by its own rule (ADR 11); the answer says so.

**Limits and scope**:
- Every list an agent sends or gets back is bounded at 50 (ADR 8). Each operation has its own constant.
- A hosted script's key (ADR 25) reaches everything here except cancel-all and close-all, which act on the whole sandbox, strategies' orders and positions included.

## Consequences

- An agent can check a set's margin, place it as one set, look at the book of its thinnest leg, move a stop, read the fills, and cancel and close everything, each in one call, with every step in the audit log.
- A strategy's runner reads what it holds outside the sandbox's write lock. A close landing between that read and the runner's own exit would make the exit open a reverse position. The window is short and the result is visible in the order book; we accept it rather than lock across the runner.
- The margin preview counts the real broker account's open positions, not the sandbox's. With an empty real account this makes no difference.
- Margin needs a live broker session. Unlike the sandbox's own checks, it fails with the reconnect message when the session has expired.
- Close-all and cancel-all don't stop strategies. A user who wants everything to stay flat stops or kills the strategies too.
