# 41. The desk manager: one agent runs the paper desk

Status: Accepted. Supersedes ADR 29's review schedules (reviews by hand stay). Builds on ADR 39 (the brain) and ADR 40 (chats). Amended by ADR 42: the wake message (a digest in tags), today's figures, two choices for its looks, the folder context.

## Context

Strategies ran on paper unattended (ADR 21, ADR 22), but nobody decided what ran each day, with how much, or what to do when one broke. The user set review schedules per strategy by hand (ADR 29), guessed their thresholds, and still heard about a bad run only after it ended. They wanted one agent to run the desk for them: pick what runs, give each strategy capital and a loss line, come back when something breaks, stop what's failing, trade on its own, and say why. The user should only have to read what it did and change what they disagree with.

The agent stays the user's own Claude Code or Codex (ADR 29, ADR 40). The question was how it wakes, what it may touch, where its numbers live, and who wins when the user and the agent disagree.

## Decision

**The manager is a chat, one thread a trading day.**
- Its thread lives in the folder `manager`, with the trading date as its subject, titled "Desk, Tue 22 Sep". Each wake is one turn of that thread: an agent job of kind `chat` whose trigger is `manager-wake:<why>`, run by the chat lane like any message (ADR 40). The day's turns share one harness session, so it remembers its morning.
- Its folder context names the day, the `manage-desk` skill in `labs/` (both copies), the user's numbers standing, and that every turn ends with `set_next_wake`.
- Its harness, model, effort, spend cap and time limit come from Settings › Agents › Manager, through the same resolve rule as every folder (ADR 40).
- A chat started by the user can't be filed in the manager's folder; the manager's threads are the daemon's.

**It wakes on its own.** Each minute the daemon asks `queue_due_manager_wake`, which decides from `core/agents/manager.py`'s `wake_due`, in this order:
- **Off, a holiday, or before the plan time:** nothing.
- **The plan:** 15 minutes before the session opens (09:00 on a normal day; a special session moves it), when the day has no thread yet, or its first turn left no conversation to resume.
- **Waiting:** while a turn is open, nothing new is added; what broke meanwhile wakes it after.
- **The cap:** at most `wakes_per_day` turns a day (40 by default, 1 to 200), counted across the day's threads.
- **A retry:** a turn that failed, timed out, couldn't start, was lost or refused is tried again for the same reason after 5 minutes, and every 30 minutes from the third failure in a row. Its message says what failed, then repeats the wake.
- **Something broke:** a new audit entry since `last_audit_id` that needs it (`broke`): a strategy stopped by its kill switch, a combined stop, its daily loss limit, lost prices, a failed recovery or an error; a script that failed, hit a limit, was lost or couldn't start; the broker session expiring; a refused order; a risk breach. What the manager did itself never wakes it. Entries that break nothing are passed over between wakes. Turning it on, and each plan, skip what came before.
- **A line crossed:** a strategy's or the desk's net today newly at or below its stop. Once until it recovers, so a line that recovers and crosses again wakes it again.
- **Its own time** (`set_next_wake`: today, at least 2 minutes ahead, before the close wake).
- **The close:** 5 minutes after the session closes, once. A failed close wake is retried.
- **The fallback:** in session with no time set, once an hour since its last look.

Each wake's message says why it woke and the facts it needs: up to 10 broken things named, the rest counted; each crossed line with its figure and stop.

**Its numbers: lines.** `manager_lines` holds a capital and a stop per line: `desk`, `own` (its own trades), `strategy:<id>`, `script:<id>`. Each has a reason, who set it and when.
- `set_manager_numbers` replaces capital and stop together, up to 50 lines a call. Capital is never negative, a stop never positive, and the manager must give a reason.
- **The user wins.** A change recorded as `manager:` (its chat key, through `recorded_as`, ADR 35) is the manager's; any other caller's is the user's (`set_by: you`). The manager's change to a line the user set keeps the user's numbers and stores the manager's beside them (`kept_yours`), so it can say it disagrees. `clear_manager_override` hands a line back, with the numbers the manager last wanted.
- Its lines act only when it looks. Each strategy's own limits (ADR 19) still act at once.

**The plan.** `set_manager_plan` stores the day's plan with the brain's lessons it relied on (`lesson_ids`, refused when unknown). The skill records each lesson's use with purpose `manage` (ADR 39).

**What its key reaches.** A manager turn runs with a chat key (`chat:<thread>`): everything a chat reaches (ADR 40), recorded as `manager:<harness>:<thread>` and shown as Manager. It may also call `start_review` on a strategy (`scopes.refusal`'s `is_manager_key`); it can't stop jobs, start debriefs, turn itself on or off, or wake itself (`set_manager`, `wake_manager`: the user's only).

**The user's controls.** `set_manager` turns it on or off and sets wakes a day. Off stops its wakes; a turn already running finishes, and nothing it started is stopped. `wake_manager` (Look now) adds a turn at once; it's refused while off, while a turn is open, or at the cap, and works after the close wake.

**Telling the user.** Its state (`manager_state`, one row) records when it last woke, its next look, its plan, the audit entries already judged and the lines crossed.
- `ManagerWoke`, `ManagerNumbersChanged`, `ManagerPlanSet` and `ManagerSwitched` are audited.
- `ManagerStalled` (failing: three turns failed in a row; capped: the day's wakes are used up) is notified once and raises a banner. It clears on the next pass with neither.
- A wake turn that finished isn't notified (`AgentJobEnded.trigger`), and is a record in the web app: forty a day would flood Slack. A failed one still is.

**Review schedules go.** `schedule_review`, `unschedule_review`, their routes and the daemon's check are removed: the manager judges when a strategy needs a review. `strategies.review_schedule` stays as an unused column (ADR 18). Reviews asked for by hand stay as in ADR 29.

**The web app's Manager page** (`/manager`, after Dashboard): On or Off, Look now; today after charges, capital given, its next look, looks today; its numbers, editable in place, with who set each and what the manager wanted on a line the user set; the day's decisions from its thread; the plan with its lessons. The strategy page shows what the manager gives it, and that it's reviewed when the manager judges it due.

## Consequences

- The user turns it on once; it plans, watches and settles each trading day, and every number it set has a reason the user can read and change.
- **Its lines are not a stop loss.** Between looks only each strategy's own limits act. Having the daemon hold the desk line and per-strategy capital between wakes is the next step.
- A strategy's line compares realized P&L after charges; an open run's unrealized loss doesn't cross it until the run closes. The desk's line reads the minute P&L record (ADR 34), open positions included.
- Scripts and its own trades have no per-day figure yet, so their lines never cross on their own; the manager reads them when it looks.
- It can't change a running strategy's limits (ADR 21): it stops one and starts another.
- Waking on price conditions ("NIFTY moves 1%") would need conditions checked against the feed. For now it sets its next look closer.
- Its own trades have no ledger apart from the account's, so whether it adds value over a hands-off desk can't be measured yet.
- Each wake is a CLI start and a model call. The wake cap and the folder's spend cap bound the day's cost; the retry gap keeps a failing harness from burning it.
