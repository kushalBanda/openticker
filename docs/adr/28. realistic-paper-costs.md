# 28. Realistic paper costs

Status: Accepted. Refines ADR 11.

## Context

The sandbox (ADR 11) filled every order at the last traded price and charged nothing. Paper results therefore flattered every strategy. A market buy got the last price instead of the ask. A resting limit filled the moment the market touched it, even though a real order at that price may still be waiting in the queue. And no brokerage, STT, exchange fee, SEBI fee, stamp duty or GST was paid. For an options strategy that trades several legs a day, the charges alone can decide whether it makes or loses money.

OpenAlgo's sandbox has the same gap. Its portfolio backtester models costs as a list of rules, each charged on turnover, on one side, or per order, with an optional cap and a flag for whether GST applies. We follow that shape.

Kite offers a virtual contract note (`POST /charges/orders`) that returns the broker's own charges for an order. Calling it on every fill would put a network call inside the sandbox's write lock and make every paper fill depend on a live session.

## Decision

**Fills pay the book.**
- A market order pays the other side of the quote: a buy the best ask, a sell the best bid.
- Without a book (a thin contract, or a streamed tick, which carries only the last price), the order pays the last price moved `SANDBOX_SLIPPAGE_TICKS` ticks against it (default 1). A sell never fills below one tick.
- A LIMIT placed through the market fills at once at the market price, which is at least as good as its limit.
- A resting LIMIT, or an armed SL, fills only when the market trades through its price (a buy below it, a sell above it), and then at its price. At its price exactly, it may be behind others in the queue.
- A triggered SL-M fills at the tick moved against it.
- Closing a position (`close_position`, close-all) pays the book like any market order.
- Each trade keeps the price the order was placed against: the last price for a market order, the limit or trigger for a resting one. The difference is the slippage a review can measure.

**Every fill pays charges.**
- Charges are rules in `src/openticker/data/charges.json`, one schedule per segment and exchange: equity delivery and intraday on NSE and BSE, futures and options on NFO and BFO.
- Each rule has a basis: `turnover`, `buy`, `sell`, or `order` (a flat amount, or a rate with a cap per order). A rule also says whether GST applies and whether the amount is rounded to the nearest rupee, as stamp duty is. An option's value is its premium.
- The rates are Zerodha's published ones. `$OPENTICKER_HOME/charges.json` replaces the file entirely, so a new budget's STT is a data change, not a release.
- On 2026-09-25 the shipped rates reproduced Kite's contract note to the paisa for delivery, intraday, futures and options on both exchanges. The tests pin those figures.
- A trade stores its charges. Funds keep a running total, and available cash is capital less used margin plus realized P&L less charges. Realized P&L, on funds and on positions, stays what the trades made before charges, so the two can be read apart.
- A trade also stores its charges item by item (the rules' keys and `gst`, summing to the total) and the P&L it realized before charges: what it closed, 0 for a fill that only opened. Both are written in the fill's own transaction, from the position it changed, so the trade book can show a fill's cost and outcome without replaying the fills before it. `get_tradebook` returns them, for today, this week or this month.
- `preview_charges` (`GET /charges/preview`) shows what one fill would pay, item by item, with the rates' source and date.
- There is no switch to turn costs off. Only the slippage ticks can be set.

**The rates are checked against the broker every day.**
- `openticker-serve` prices sample orders through Kite's contract note once on each day it runs, and `check_charge_rates` (`POST /charges/check`) does it on demand. Nothing is placed, and no fill ever waits on it.
- The samples are a buy and a sell for each segment the rates cover, at the contract's last price: RELIANCE for equity; for index derivatives, the nearest NIFTY or SENSEX future and the call nearest to that future's price.
- Because Kite classifies intraday by matching a buy against a sell (below), an intraday sample's buy and sell go in one request, so they net as a round trip. Every other buy goes in one request and every other sell in another, so no delivery or F&O order nets.
- Any item, or the total, more than a paisa apart is reported with both figures and notified (`ChargeRatesDiffer`). The fix is a corrected `$OPENTICKER_HOME/charges.json`; the check never rewrites rates by itself, because a wrong answer from the broker should not change what paper fills pay.
- A segment it can't sample (instruments not synced, no last price, a schedule with no sample rule, like a commodity one added to an override file) is skipped and says why. A check that can't run at all (broker not connected) is tried again an hour later.
- On 2026-09-25 all sixteen samples matched to the paisa.

**What is not modelled.**
- **MCX:** Kite charges commodities on quantity × price × the contract multiplier (100 barrels a lot for crude), and the instrument master does not carry the multiplier. Charging on quantity × price would be 100 times too low, so MCX fills record no charges until the multiplier is modelled.
- **Expiry settlement:** it pays no charges. STT on exercise, and assignment, are not modelled.
- **Intraday classification:** Kite classifies intraday by matching a buy against a sell on the same day, not by the product. We charge by the product. For the round trips an intraday strategy makes, the two agree.

Trades from before this change have no charges and no expected price (both NULL). They read as "before costs". Trades from before the itemised charges and per-fill realized P&L were kept have NULL in both (ADR 18). They read as not recorded, and a sum of realized P&L over trades counts them apart.

## Consequences

- A paper round trip now loses the spread and both sides' charges, as a real one does. A strategy that only broke even before costs shows as losing.
- Strategy-wide limits (ADR 19) still act on prices, not on charges. A combined stop loss of ₹3,000 is ₹3,000 of price movement; the charges come on top.
- Rates go stale when the law changes. The daily check says so within a day, with the figures to correct them; until someone does, paper fills keep paying the old rates.
- Tests whose subject isn't costs build the sandbox with no slippage and an empty charges book (`tests/fixtures/sandbox.py`). Costs are tested on their own.
