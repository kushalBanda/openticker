# 11. The sandbox is its own broker adapter

Status: Accepted. Supersedes the "single path with a sandbox/live mode" part of ADR 6.

## Context

ADR 6 made order placement sandbox-only, with sandbox and live as two modes of one `place_order` path. A mode flag keeps live code one condition away from running, and it leaves open who confirms a fill: the broker (postbacks or polling) or us.

Strategies also need a few order fields the first design lacked: which product an order is (intraday or overnight) and which strategy placed it. And a risk limit must never stop anyone getting out of a position.

## Decision

- **`SandboxBroker` is an adapter** that satisfies `BrokerPort`. It takes quotes and instruments from the real broker adapter and keeps orders, trades, positions and funds in sandbox tables. The registry hands it out for the order side. No live order path exists in the code.
- **The sandbox is the source of fills.** It fills a MARKET order at a fresh price and a resting LIMIT, SL or SL-M order when a live price crosses it, then publishes `OrderFilled`. It never fills without a fresh price: a quote whose last price falls outside its own day range is treated as stale. How a live broker confirms fills (postbacks or polling) is left to the live-order design.
- **`OrderRequest` carries `product`** (`MIS` intraday, `NRML` overnight derivatives, `CNC` delivery) with no silent default, and optional `strategy_id` and `run_id`.
- **Closing orders are never blocked by entry checks.** Whether an order closes is read from the account's net position, not declared by the caller: an order that only reduces a position skips the capital cap and needs no margin, so neither can trap a position. An order that flips a position is checked on the new side. Shape validation always applies.
- **Money is virtual.** Starting capital is ₹1 crore unless `SANDBOX_STARTING_CAPITAL` says otherwise. Opening blocks margin of the order's value divided by a leverage (intraday equity 5x, delivery 1x, futures 10x, options bought or sold 1x the premium); closing releases the margin it blocked and moves realized profit or loss into available cash. Charges and taxes are not modelled. `OPENTICKER_CAPITAL_CAP` optionally caps what one position may be worth.
- **CNC shares cannot be sold short.**
- **Every fill is one SQLite transaction** (`BEGIN IMMEDIATE`) covering order, trade, position and funds. The MCP process and the daemon both place sandbox orders, and neither may read-modify-write outside that transaction.
- **Resting orders.** LIMIT, SL and SL-M orders rest as `PENDING`, holding margin for the part that would open a position, valued at the limit (or the trigger, for SL-M). The daemon's execution engine fills them once a live price received after placement crosses them, at that price, which is at least as good as the limit. At fill time the held margin is swapped for the real margin; if the funds no longer cover it, the order is rejected and releases its hold. A LIMIT the market is already through fills at once. An SL or SL-M whose trigger is already crossed is refused, as exchanges refuse it. An SL stays armed once its trigger is crossed. `cancel_order` withdraws a pending order and releases its margin.
- **Day orders only.** A pending MIS order expires at the square-off, and any other pending order at the session's close.
- **Intraday square-off.** The daemon closes MIS positions 15 minutes before the session's close. After that, MIS orders may only reduce a position, so one can always be closed by hand. If the daemon wasn't running at the square-off, it closes any MIS position it finds open outside the intraday window as soon as it starts, at the latest price. A failed square-off is retried after 5 minutes, then twice as long each time up to an hour. A contract past its expiry can't be traded, so the square-off leaves it to settlement.
- **Expiry settlement.** Once an expiry day's close is final (15 minutes after the session ends), the daemon closes every position still open in a contract that expired that day, whatever its product. Index derivatives settle in cash on the underlying's close on expiry day: a future at that close, an option at its intrinsic value (a call at close minus strike, a put at strike minus close, never below zero). Stock F&O settle by delivery on the exchange; the sandbox settles them in cash the same way, which gives the same profit or loss without delivering shares. MCX contracts have no listed underlying here, so they settle at their own close on expiry day. Closes come from daily candles, so a daemon that was down on expiry day settles correctly when it next runs. Each settlement is recorded as an order (`triggered_by="expiry-settlement"`) and notified as `PositionSettled`. Unlike openalgo, which settles at the option's last traded price, this uses the underlying's close, which is what the exchange uses and doesn't depend on an illiquid contract having traded near the close. A MARKET order needs its exchange open (market calendar, ADR 13): outside the session the last price is yesterday's, not one anyone could trade at, so the order is rejected with when the exchange next opens.

## Consequences

- Live trading, when designed, is a new adapter behind the same port rather than a flag flipped inside an existing one.
- Resting orders fill only while the daemon (ADR 12) is running.
- Profit and loss can be attributed per strategy and per run from tagged sandbox trades.
