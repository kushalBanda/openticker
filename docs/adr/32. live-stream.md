# 32. The web app's live stream

Status: Accepted. Covers prices and their source, and the event feed.

## Context

A page of prices has to move within about a second of the market, and the page has to hear of what happens while it is open: a fill, a kill, an expired session. The daemon already streams the broker's ticks into `LatestPrices` for what it watches (ADR 13): open positions, pending orders and `OPENTICKER_WATCH`. A browser wants other instruments too (the index strip, a symbol it is looking at), and the feed re-reads its watch list only every 5 seconds. Browsers also come and go, open several tabs, and lose their connection. Agents mostly trade over stdio MCP: a separate process per session with its own event bus, whose events never reach the server's bus. Every process writes the one `audit_log`, inline (ADR 10).

## Decision

**One WebSocket per tab, `/api/v1/stream`**, browser session only (ADR 31; close code 4401 when it ends, 4403 from a foreign origin).
- On connect the server sends `hello` (server time, newest audit id) and `status`: the broker, whether its session is live and when it expires, the last tick's time, whether the market is open, and one state: `live` (a tick within 10 s), `quiet` (market open, no tick for 10 s), `closed`, `no-broker`. Status is re-checked every second and sent when it changes.
- The page sends `subscribe` / `unsubscribe` with the instruments it shows (at most 200 per connection) and `ping` (answered `pong`). An unknown instrument is reported as an `error` message; a bad message is answered, never fatal.
- A new subscription gets its price at once: the latest tick, or, before the first one, a broker quote. That quote also gives the previous close, fetched once per instrument per day, which every `tick` carries a change from (`+13.70 (+0.47%)`, as Kite shows it).
- Every 250 ms each connection is sent the prices that changed since it was last sent them: at most four a second per instrument. A price that moves and moves back within one window is not sent.
- Subscriptions are counted across all connections. The feed streams every instrument some page wants (`watched_instruments` gains a browser source), and a new one sets the feed's `wake` event so the feed subscribes on its next pass, within a second, instead of up to 5 s later. A closed socket releases everything it held.
- **Events are the audit log's tail.** While any page is open, the hub reads `audit_log` for entries after its cursor every 0.5 s (one indexed query, whoever has pages open) and sends each to every page as `event`, the same entry `get_audit_log` returns, with its `source` (ADR 35). A page gets the entries after the one its `hello` named, once each. With no page open the tail stops; the next page starts from the newest entry.
- Every minute a connection re-checks its session, which also keeps it alive; a session signed out elsewhere closes the socket with 4401.

**In the browser**, components subscribe through `usePrice`; the connection batches what a render subscribes into one message and counts keys across components. It reconnects with backoff and resubscribes; after three failed attempts it shows prices from `POST /quotes` every 2 s, marked as not streamed, until the socket is back.

**What the page does with an event** is decided by its type (`ui/src/lib/events.ts`): must-act (a strategy killed, or stopped by its daily loss limit, stale prices, a failed recovery or an error; the broker session expired) raises a banner and a red toast, and puts a dot on the tab's icon until read; worth-knowing (fills, rejections, other stops, closed legs, settlements, script exits, finished reviews) a toast, shown for 5 s, at most three at once; both are listed in the bell, the last 50, with a count of those not yet read. The rest (orders placed, changed or cancelled, syncs, starts) only in Activity. Every event refetches the reads it changed (a fill: positions, orders, funds, trades). The user's own order that fills within 5 s of being placed is not toasted again: the window that placed it said so. The bell's read mark and dismissed banners are kept in the browser; a new browser starts with nothing unread. After a reconnect whose `hello` is past the last event seen, the page refetches everything.

## Consequences

- A price on screen is at most about 250 ms behind `LatestPrices`.
- Opening a page never waits on the broker except for the first quote of an instrument that day.
- The server holds no per-browser state beyond open sockets: a restart costs one reconnect.
- An agent's order shows on the page within about a second, whichever process placed it, at the cost of two small queries a second while a page is open. Events are up to half a second late.
