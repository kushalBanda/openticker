# 43. The desk manager's past days are read back, not stored

Status: Accepted. Extends ADR 41 (the Manager page, its lines) and ADR 42 (`DayFigure`, the two tiers, a turn's own cost).

## Context

The Manager page (ADR 41) shows only today. A user asking "what did it do on Tuesday, and how did that day go?" had to open the day's chat in Agents, the brain's day record and the Activity log, and piece together which numbers stood when. Each of those already keeps its part of the day: every line write publishes `ManagerNumbersChanged` (the manager's set, the user's set, the manager's wish kept beside the user's, and handing a line back), the plan is `ManagerPlanSet`, the day's chat is a manager thread whose subject is the date, every turn records its own cost and choice (ADR 42), the P&L record keeps each closed day and the minutes of the last 30 (ADR 34), and a strategy's day is its fills that day (ADR 42).

## Decision

**A past day is rebuilt from what's kept.** No table, column or snapshot is added. `get_manager_day(day)` and `list_manager_days(start, end)` are two read-only tools with REST routes (`GET /manager/days/{date}`, `GET /manager/days?start=&end=`), reached wherever `get_manager` is. They're separate from `get_manager` because today is live and editable and a past day is neither. A day that isn't a trading day, or hasn't happened, is refused with the reason (`ManagerDayError`, 404); a range over 60 days, or backwards, is a 422. Today is a valid day and comes back `live`.

**The lines are as they stood at the close.** `lines_at` folds every `ManagerNumbersChanged` up to that day's session close (`session_hours(day).closes_at`, 15:30), skipping the manager's wishes kept beside the user's number. The close look sets tomorrow's numbers after 15:30: those changes are listed in the line's story, marked as after the close, but aren't folded, so the day shows what governed its session. A line first set after the close is listed but didn't stand. Each line keeps its changes that day, oldest first, with who and why. The fold reads the whole log up to that day's end, oldest first, at most 10,000 entries, with a warning logged when the limit is reached; an entry whose key no longer parses is skipped. A line crossed if it stood with a stop and its figure that day was at or below it.

**The money is the P&L record.** The desk's figure is that day's `daily_pnl` row as a `DayFigure`, else the last minute recorded that day, else none ("not recorded", never 0). A strategy's figure is `day_figure` over its fills that day.

**The rest is the day's thread.** Looks are the day's wake turns; cost is every turn's own; the models are grouped by tier, model and effort, decisive first. The plan is the newest `ManagerPlanSet` for that trading date. "It ran" means a manager thread exists for that day, so a day it was switched on but never woke reads as off. The page shows the day's newest thread's decisions, as today's does. Debriefed means the brain's day note has a headline.

**On the page:** a tape of about six weeks of trading days under the header, each a bar of that day's net from the zero line and a dot where it ran, with the arrow keys stepping between them and a date picker for any day further back, which re-centres the tape. `/manager/<date>` opens a past day in the same tiles, read only: its numbers at the close with each line's story, how it ran (its looks on the session's rail, what they ran on, what it spent), the plan, its decisions and a link to the day in the brain. `/manager/<today>` goes to `/manager`.

## Consequences

- Nothing new is written, so every day the manager ran before this change can be read back, and there's no second record to drift from the first.
- Each past-day read folds the change log from the start. That's tens of rows a day; if years of use make it slow, a snapshot at each close is the way out, and the 10,000 limit says so in the log before then.
- A day whose `daily_pnl` row is missing and whose minutes have aged out of the 30 days shows no desk figure.
- A day that retried into a second thread shows only the newest thread's decisions; its looks and cost still count every thread.
- The desk's figure includes what was open at the close; a strategy's doesn't (ADR 42), so the lines won't sum to the desk exactly on a day positions were carried.
