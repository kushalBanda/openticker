# 16. Option chain and Greeks conventions

Status: Accepted

## Context

An option chain is only useful if its implied volatilities and Greeks mean what a trader expects. Several choices change every number: which pricing model, which underlying price, when an option expires, how time is counted, and what units Greeks are reported in. Indian index futures trade at a premium to spot, so pricing options off spot skews every implied volatility and delta.

## Decision

- **Black-76 on the forward.** Indian index and stock options are priced as options on the forward. The forward is implied by put-call parity at the at-the-money strike, `F = K + (C - P) * e^(rT)`, so no futures quote is needed. When either at-the-money leg has no price, the underlying's own price is used instead.
- **At the money** is the listed strike nearest the underlying's last price; a tie goes to the lower strike. Labels count listed strikes from it: below at-the-money a call is `ITM<n>` and a put `OTM<n>`, above it the reverse.
- **Expiry** is 15:30 exchange time on the expiry date. Time to expiry is calendar time on a 365-day year, floored at 0.0001 years (about 53 minutes) so the model stays finite on expiry afternoon. Once 15:30 passes, that expiry is dropped and the next one is the default.
- **Interest rate** defaults to 0, settable per call.
- **Units:** implied volatility in percent (annualized), theta per calendar day, vega per 1 point of volatility, rho per 1 point of interest rate.
- **Implied volatility** is solved by bisection between 0.01% and 500%. A price at or below intrinsic value (no time value), or above what an option can be worth, has no implied volatility. Such a contract gets the `intrinsic` model: delta is the full discounted delta in the money and 0 out of it, every other Greek is 0. A contract with no price at all gets no Greeks.
- **Which options belong to an underlying:** a stock's options carry its own symbol; indices are listed under a different name than their options (`NIFTY 50` has `NIFTY` options, `NIFTY BANK` has `BANKNIFTY`), so indices are mapped explicitly in `core/options/underlyings.py`. Option symbols are matched against the full `<name><DDMMMYY><strike><CE|PE>` shape (ADR 4), so `NIFTY` never picks up `NIFTYNXT50`.
- The math is pure Python with the standard library only (`core/options/`); the chain quotes its contracts in one batched broker call.

## Consequences

- At a given strike, the call's and put's implied volatilities agree (checked against a live NIFTY chain), because both are priced off the same forward.
- An index added to the exchange needs one line in the mapping before its chain works; the error message lists the indices that are mapped.
- These are the same conventions strategy legs use to pick strikes relative to the market (ADR 14).
